Method, and what this cannot tell you

Sample size. Every score is shrunk toward the population mean in proportion to how little evidence supports it — empirical Bayes (James–Stein), adj = (n·x̄ + k·μ)/(n + k). A 7.00 from three students lands near average; a 6.85 from 99 barely moves.

What n is. The portal's detailed score reports carry true respondent counts and full rating distributions, but they are generated on demand and could not be retrieved without overloading the server. n here is the number of written comments — a lower bound on respondents, not the rating count. Sound as a relative weight, conservative as an absolute one. Response rates are unknown.

Course worth is course-level and repeats across co-instructors, so it is de-duplicated to one observation per course-term before averaging. Instructor effectiveness is instructor-level and is not.

Consistency is the SD across terms — computable for 101 of 220 pairs. Trend needs 3+ terms and exists for 14; treat it as a hint. Quality per effort is the residual after regressing adjusted effectiveness on difficulty and group-project load.

Bid points are absent because they do not exist. Haas states that "no record of previous bid point cutoffs is captured or shared from year to year."

Instructor priors. Separate from the course scores, each instructor is summarised across their entire record in the 2021–2026 evaluation file — 4,867 section-evaluations, 471 instructors, 16 terms, every program (MBA, EWMBA, UGBA, XMBA, MFE, PhD). Percentiles compare against all faculty with 3+ observations. This is what makes never-evaluated Fall 2026 courses assessable at all, and it is deliberately a weak prior: it says something about the teacher, nothing about the course.

Coverage. Spring 2025, Fall 2025, Spring 2026 for course-level scores and comments; 2021–2026 for instructor priors. MBA-prefixed courses. 158 of 371 sections are cross-listed with EWMBA or UGBA, so those comments are not purely from the FTMBA population.

What 4,867 evaluations say

Patterns in five years of Berkeley Haas course evaluations — the things you can only see by pooling every program, not just the courses you might take.

How to read these. Every panel below pools all six programs (MBA, EWMBA, UGBA, XMBA, MFE, PhD) across 16 terms. These are observational patterns, not experiments: a difference between groups can reflect which courses ended up in each group rather than any causal effect. Where a result is statistically weak I say so on the panel rather than burying it.
