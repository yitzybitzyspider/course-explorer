How to read this

Sample size is baked in. Every score is shrunk toward the population mean in proportion to how little evidence supports it — a 7.00 from three students lands near average; a 6.85 from 99 barely moves. The per-student rating SD behind that adjustment is 0.995, measured on all 150 elective score reports.

Course worth is course-level and repeats across co-instructors, so it is de-duplicated to one observation per section before averaging. Instructor effectiveness is instructor-level.

Hours per week are students' own answers to report item 13, available for 188 pairs. Where no detailed report exists, the effort column falls back to difficulty and says so.

Bid points are absent because they do not exist — Haas states it keeps no record of previous clearing cutoffs from year to year.

Almost nothing you'd use to pick a course actually predicts anything.

Five years of evaluations, 52,381 student comments and 305 detailed score reports, read against the assumptions people bid on. Difficulty, workload, popularity, novelty — tested one at a time. Most did not survive.

Method & limits

Scores are shrunk toward the population mean in proportion to sample size (empirical Bayes). The per-student rating SD behind that adjustment is 0.995, measured on all 150 elective score reports — an earlier figure of 1.35 was an assumption drawn from four, and it made every interval too wide.

Course worth is course-level and repeats across co-instructors; it is de-duplicated to one observation per section before averaging. Correlations are Pearson. These are observational patterns, not experiments: a difference between groups can reflect which courses ended up in each group rather than any causal effect.

Historical bid points appear nowhere because Haas does not retain them — its own bidding page states that no record of previous cutoffs is captured or shared from year to year.
